The chain library: blocks, transactions, and kernels for nockchain

Status: NORMATIVE for the Jock-side types and kernels. The format authority is nockchain's tx-engine at the pinned snapshot (§3) — where this document and the snapshot disagree, the snapshot wins and this document has a bug.

This document carries no chronology. The phase history, the surveys it rests on, and the questions that were resolved to produce these rules are docs/record/chain-arc.md; what is still ahead is docs/ROADMAP.md.

Rules carry flat C-n ids, append-only. Each names what pins it — a corpus vector, or the lane that checks it, since most of this document's claims are about agreement with a vendored artifact and no corpus row can hold that. A rule with neither says ⟨none — UNPINNED⟩ so the gap is countable.

1. Posture: representations and calls

C-1 · A Jock kernel is a PURE STATE MACHINE at the NockApp driver boundary. Drivers own the world — gRPC, files, network, clocks. This library owns representations, the chain types as Jock types constructed and validated in-kernel, and calls, effects requesting driver actions and pokes receiving driver-delivered data. Nothing in Jock fetches, connects or persists. ⟨t-kern-fx, t-kern-fx-res; lane tools/chain-e2e.sh⟩

C-2 · INBOUND: a watcher kernel never asks for blocks; a driver pokes them in. The precedent is the wallet's update-balance-grpc poke — the kernel is told the chain view the driver chose to share, and never derives its own. ⟨lane tools/wallet.sh⟩

C-3 · OUTBOUND: the driver effect enums are the call vocabulary — nockapp.Effect for exit and markdown, wallet.Effect for file and nockchain-grpc (runtime.md R-83). Each case's erasure matches a shipped driver's scan exactly, union erasure being the tagged-cell convention and therefore free. ⟨t-kern-fx, t-kern-fx-res, t-kern-fx-resb⟩

C-4 · SERVED: the signer kernel is a driver making calls to US. KernelSigner pokes create-tx and we answer with an effect list, via poke_timeout — one poke in, complete effects out, which is exactly the kernel model, with no run-loop and no %exit handling on either side. ⟨lane tools/signer.sh⟩

C-5 · The ONLY imports rather than representations are crypto and chain-container primitives (§5) — computation, not I/O. ⟨t-cimp-nest, t-cimp-annot⟩

2. Phases and what each one owns

The phase history — when each landed, what was proven, and the findings that narrowed them — is docs/record/chain-arc.md; what is still ahead is docs/ROADMAP.md. The rules here are the scope boundaries themselves, which are normative because they say which document owns a given claim.

C-11 · The PACKAGE is the delivery mechanism, not the boot surface. pkg/chain-core.hoon is the curated core — z-ops, tip5's hash arms, and to-b58 mirroring the engine's own composition verbatim — tools/pkg.sh builds chain.jam, and libs.py's *.jam pattern supplies it. A noun digest IS Hash's erasure, so the hash wrappers are pass-throughs. ⟨t-cimp-nest, t-cimp-annot, t-cimp-seal; lane tools/pkg.sh⟩

C-12 · PHASE 1 owns the read-only surface: Jock types for page/local-page, block-id, coinbase-split and the tx-id set; a watcher kernel whose state is the two-map store the node itself uses; pokes delivering blocks and txs; peeks for height, tip and summaries; and nockapp.Effect.markdown summaries out. No crypto beyond hash display and optional digest re-verification. ⟨lane tools/examples.sh; lane tools/watch.sh⟩

C-13 · A BLOCK COMMITS TX IDS ONLY, never bodies, which is why the store is two maps and not one: rendering a block requires the second lookup and there is no shortcut. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh⟩

C-14 · PHASE 2 owns construction: the full tx mold family (§4), seed/spend/raw-tx construction in Jock, IDs computed through the pinned hash arms, and the fixture corpus (§7) proving noun-identical results against tx-engine itself. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh⟩

C-15 · PHASE 3 owns the SIGNING PATH ONLY. A Jock wallet kernel accepts a key, accepts a create-tx cause, signs, and emits the signed envelope — serving that slice of the wallet contract (§6.2) exactly. Key DERIVATION is phase 4. ⟨lane tools/signer.sh⟩

C-16 · "Drop-in with zero Rust-side changes" is retained as the EVENTUAL claim and is NOT phase 3's exit condition. It is roughly the size of phases 1 and 2 combined: the stock wallet is ~4,800 lines of Hoon (wallet.hoon 2,289, utils 1,160, types 680, tx-builder 595, s10 56) over a ~35-variant cause union, plus /common/bip39 and /common/slip10. ⟨none — UNPINNED⟩

C-17 · Substitution is a FILE SWAP. The harness that can gate a lane is crates/nockchain-wallet/src/tests.rs, which boots kernels_open_wallet::KERNEL = include_bytes!(env!("WALLET_JAM_PATH")) → assets/wal.jam. The crates/tx-driver crate — whose KERNEL_SIGNER_SPEC.md is the document §6.2 was written from — lives on the tx-driver branch, not master and not at the vendored anchor, so it cannot gate a lane today. ⟨lane tools/wallet.sh⟩

The wallet suite is not a signer suite, which is why passing it would demonstrate the signing format not at all. Of its 22 kernel tests, 16 are active and 6 are #[ignore] stubs whose bodies are // TODO and Ok(()) — and the ignored six are precisely the tx-shaped ones (test_spend_single_sig_format, test_spend_multisig_format, test_make_tx_from_draft, test_show_tx, test_import_keys). The 16 that run gate on key derivation, the planner, and the v0→v1 migration. tx-driver is where phase 4 should end up, and it answers the coverage gap §7 records: sign.rs::validate_signed checks a signed transaction against the plan that asked for it — signatures, inputs, outputs and fee — which is exactly the "does a Jock-emitted tx validate" test we do not have.

C-18 · PHASE 4 owns key management: keygen (argon2 entropy → mnemonic), import-seed-phrase (bip39 to-seed → SLIP-10 from-seed), derive-child, the active-signers peek, and the v0→v1 migration path. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh⟩

C-19 · Phase 4 is SEPARABLE because %import-keys takes a raw coil, [%0|%1 [key cc]]. A signing key can be injected with no derivation machinery at all, which is what lets phase 3 reach the full signing path without any of phase 4. ⟨lane tools/signer.sh⟩

C-20 · The derivation libraries are vendored at the same anchor and validated the same way. bip39, bip39-english and slip10 sit beside the format snapshot, and zoon.hoon at that rev was re-hashed to the recorded SNAPSHOT.txt value before any of them was trusted. The package carries four pass-through arms — toseed, fromseed, derivekey, mnemonic — mirroring /apps/wallet/lib/s10, which is app code and so is not vendored; lib/chainkey.jock puts the molds back on as Keyc, Coil and the derivation wrappers. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh; lane ci:pins⟩

C-21 · HARDENING IS AN INDEX CONVENTION, not a flag. +derive-child:utils receives an index with 2^31 already added and infers hardening from its magnitude. A wrapper that added the bit twice, or not at all, would still produce a valid-looking key — only the hardened/unhardened row pair catches it. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh⟩

C-22 · The derivation fixtures use the WALLET SUITE'S OWN test seed phrase, so a divergence in them is a divergence from the value the Rust tests would compute. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh⟩

4. Type mapping (normative table)

C-23 · A Hoon $% tagged union maps to a Jock union ONLY where the Hoon tags are ALPHABETIC. A Jock union tags with the case name as @tas, so %leaf/%hash/%list erase identically while spend's %0/%1 do not — and $tex has no singleton spelling (language.md L-93), so a field cannot be typed as the literal 1 either. A numeric-tagged mold carries the tag as an ordinary leading version: @ field and is left un-joined. ⟨t-cimp-match, t-cimp-nest⟩

C-24 · A Hoon $^ head-shape union DOES have a Jock spelling. The judgment's cell side names head shape as well as head tags: pair, where the head is a cell, is disjoint from atom and from any tag set, so an untagged union of a struct-headed member and an atom-headed one is admissible and its members are told apart at runtime. This is what makes page, nnote, local-page and lock-merkle-proof expressible. ⟨t-nest-orin-p, t-nest-oror-p, t-join-sub-cell-or-p, t-join-sub-or-cell-p⟩

C-25 · A ONE-FIELD struct erases to its field and is therefore ATOM-HEADED, so $^ alternatives must differ in head shape at two or more fields. This caveat survived both earlier readings of C-24 and is the part that bites. ⟨t-join-sub-null-or-p, t-nest-orout-n⟩

C-26 · C-24 does not affect the transaction core, which never needed it: raw-tx v1 is a single version ($: version=%1 id spends, not a dispatch at all) and spend is $%, so Source, Name, Seed, Seeds, Spend, Spends and RawTx are all expressible without it. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh⟩

C-27 · z-map/z-set become boot data nominals ZMap<K, V> and ZSet<T> — the same treap shape as Map and Set, so reading by data-unrolled match works identically, but TIP5-hash-ordered. Construction goes through the pinned z-* arms over the vendored zoon doors, NEVER through the mug-ordered mput/sput family. ⟨t-cimp-nest, t-cimp-annot; lane tools/pkg.sh⟩

C-28 · The treap SHAPE is consensus-critical, every container hashing as its literal tree and IDs baking the shape in. So Jock-native z-construction is deferred until the tip5 FFI story is jetted — wrappers-first, on the Map/Set precedent (runtime.md R-37). ⟨lane tools/molds.sh⟩

C-29 · hash — which is also block-id and tx-id — is struct Hash { a: @, b: @, c: @, d: @, e: @ } of %based-guarded belts: five Goldilocks field elements, under the guard the language already has. A belt elsewhere is @ with the same guard wherever field-validity is an invariant, as in note-data leaves and hash limbs. ⟨t-as-grd-sub, t-nest-guard-wide-n; lane tools/molds.sh⟩

C-30 · Name keeps the dead pact slot as a zero pad, so its key noun erases [first last 0] on the wire. The slot is format, not data. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh⟩

C-31 · The tx id is DERIVED, not supplied — content-addressed from the spends. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh; lane tools/chain-e2e.sh⟩

The fixture-parity boundary, phase 2 proper. The real compute-id is hash-hashable:tip5 over the spends' structured hashable DSL, which needs tip5 serialization arms the package does not yet export. So hashnoun gives a real, self-consistent content hash but not the consensus id, and witness stays * until phase-3 signatures. z-ordering is self-consistent, the put and has arms sharing the vendored z-doors, so membership is exact even where the absolute id is not.

5. Hashing, serialization, and the FFI surface

C-34 · WIRE IS JAM OF THE EXACT NOUN. There is no custom byte layout anywhere, and size is (met 0 (jam n)). Fixture-identical therefore means noun-identical, which Jock's erasure discipline is built for. ⟨t-abi-jam; lane tools/molds.sh⟩

C-35 · IDENTITY IS THE TIP5 HASHABLE DSL, not the jam. Every ID composes hash-noun-varlen (noun → digest via leaf-sequence plus Dyck word) and hash-ten-cell (digest pair → digest), with domain-separating leaf+%tag siblings. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh⟩

C-36 · A CONTAINER HASHES AS ITS LITERAL TREAP TREE, which is the load-bearing half of C-35 and the reason C-28 defers Jock-native construction. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh⟩

C-37 · SIGNING AND IDENTITY HASHING DIVERGE ON seed. output-source is excluded from identity, to break a cycle, and included in what is signed. ⟨lane tools/signer.sh⟩

C-38 · The chain arms do NOT join the prelude, which stays the universal hoon-138 surface (runtime.md R-81). They arrive as a library package: a def-env fragment [types + hlib] — the data-nominal registrations (ZMap, ZSet, Hash, later the tx primitive layer) together with the import binding, which is the compiled curated core over the vendored snapshot plus its pinned signatures. ⟨t-cimp-nest, t-cimp-annot, t-cimp-seal⟩

C-39 · TYPES TRAVEL WITH ARMS because the signatures reference the nominals. A package is precisely the piece of def.env a library contributes, and import chain; resolves it exactly as import hoon; does — imports.def was always plural. ⟨t-cimp-annot, t-run-import-mix⟩

C-40 · A package is built by a dedicated tool and supplied via libs.cfg. Compile the curated core under the pinned toolchain, pair it with the signature list, jam it; +boot-env merges it at construction. The core compiler carries no chain code. This is the module shape of docs/imports.md §8 — types.md records it under S14's closed Jock-native-modules item, and S11.2 is the typing half — instantiated for Hoon libraries first; Jock-compiled packages later reuse the format with only the core's provenance changing. ⟨lane tools/pkg.sh; lane ci:pins⟩

C-41 · The import story is a PHASE-1 PREREQUISITE, the printer needing tip5 digest verification and b58 display, and it raises the stakes on closure slimming — an importing kernel embeds the package core in its captured spine. ⟨lane tools/watch.sh⟩

C-43 · The schnorr transcript is CODEBASE-SPECIFIC, not BIP-340, and is never reimplemented. ⟨lane tools/signer.sh⟩

C-44 · READING a z-container is pure Jock — a data-unrolled match on the treap shape, ordering-blind — so the block printer walks tx-ids and coinbase-split natively. Only construction requires the pinned arms. ⟨t-cimp-match; lane tools/examples.sh⟩

6. The kernels

6.1 The watcher (phase 1)

C-45 · State mirrors the node's own two-map split (C-13):

struct WatchState {
  blocks: ZMap<Hash, LocalPage>,   // by block-id
  txs: ZMap<Hash, RawTx>,          // by tx-id
  tip: Hash?,                      // heaviest seen
}

⟨lane tools/watch.sh; lane tools/examples.sh⟩

C-46 · Each poke is a tagged union case, driver-delivered. block(LocalPage) verifies check-digest under optional strict mode, stores, updates tip by accumulated-work comparison, and emits a markdown summary; tx(RawTx) recomputes the id and stores. Peeks are height, tip and block(id). Effects are markdown only. ⟨t-kern-fx; lane tools/watch.sh⟩

C-47 · Pokes arrive ARVO-SHAPED and unwrap through an Ovum mirror struct — mold dispatch rather than axis arithmetic. Kernel state holds the imported nominal, molded by load's as! through %mold-gen. ⟨t-cimp-annot, t-kern-load; lane tools/examples.sh⟩

C-48 · A match mixing quiet and effectful arms needs an annotated empty list. Arms mixing (~, self) with ([fx], st') refuse %join-fail; the quiet arms need let none: List<nockapp.Effect> = ~ to join. This is the cons/fxs idiom (language.md L-94's +join rule seen from a kernel). ⟨t-join-fail-n, t-join-sub-null-or-p⟩

6.2 The signer (phase 3)

C-49 · lib/chaintx.jock carries the witness family — SchnorrPubkey, SchnorrSignature, Pkh, Tim, Hax, LockPrimitive, SpendCondition, MerkProof, LockMerkleProof, PkhSignature, Witness — and every mold is checked against the engine's own bunts. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh⟩

C-50 · A Jock signer emits %full lock-merkle-proofs ONLY. lock-merkle-proof is a $^ head-shape union — the stub's head a cell, the full case's the atom %full — which Jock can spell (C-24). A signer does not need to: it PRODUCES proofs rather than accepting them, the engine's own bunt IS the %full case, and the stub is the legacy shape whose hardcoded digest §7 preserves rather than fixes. Only %full is declared; READING a stub proof needs the head-shape judgment. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh⟩

C-51 · Signing arms must be PASS-THROUGHS to the engine's schnorr. A hand-written signature scheme would be the one unacceptable thing to put in this tree. ⟨lane tools/signer.sh⟩

C-52 · A Jock signature is BYTE-IDENTICAL to the engine's for the same (sk, digest), so signing is exact-equality testable and not merely verify-testable: the schnorr nonce is hash-varlen(pubkey ‖ m ‖ sk) — fully deterministic, no randomness. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh; lane tools/signer.sh⟩

C-53 · The redaction discipline is that every String-producing arm takes only types that cannot hold a key — and it does NOT refuse a leaking program. Rewrite a render arm to take the keyring and print its secret and it compiles cleanly. What the discipline buys is that a leak requires changing a SIGNATURE, a visible and reviewable edit, rather than slipping into a body. The enforcement is the lane. ⟨lane tools/signer.sh⟩

C-54 · The redaction adversary searches TWICE: for the key as a value, and for its decimal rendering as a substring, each with a control row proving the search finds what it is looking for. One search is not enough — interpolation renders an atom as decimal text inside a cord, which a noun search cannot see. ⟨lane tools/signer.sh⟩

C-55 · The v0 MIGRATION path is not implemented in Jock. It is legacy-only, a one-way sweep of pre-v1 notes, and a Jock signer that never has to spend a v0 note never needs it. The version dispatch is not the obstacle — the stock wallet chooses between spend-0 and spend-1 with ?: (levy notes |=(=nnote ?=(^ -.nnote))), a cell head meaning a v0 note, which is the head-shape test of C-24 and is writable at runtime with hoon.iscell. The obstacle is the construction. ⟨t-abi-iscell-cell; lane tools/wallet.sh⟩

C-56 · The kernel builds %1 spends UNCONDITIONALLY. Handed an all-v0 input set it would emit a v1 spend, which is wrong rather than refused — the honest consequence of eliding C-55's path. ⟨lane tools/wallet.sh⟩

C-57 · The state has FIVE FIELDS with the constants LAST, because the driver's constants peek reads AXIS 31 and a right-nested five-tuple puts its last field there. A state-layout change reddens the lane exactly as it would redden the wallet suite. ⟨lane tools/signer.sh⟩

C-58 · The two key peeks compute what the stock wallet computes — hash:schnorr-pubkey of the point ch-scal derives from the active secret — and the absent case REFUSES rather than answering a zero hash, which would be a well-formed value meaning nothing. ⟨lane tools/signer.sh⟩

A row written against the implementation instead of against the engine proves only that the implementation is itself. /master-signing-key and /signing-keys first shipped as placeholders, and contract-peek-master-key compared the kernel's placeholder against a hand-written copy of the same placeholder — green, and a test of nothing. Every other row in this lane and in molds.sh compares to a vendored arm; these two did not, and that is exactly why they passed while wrong.

C-59 · The kernel serves five causes and four peeks. examples/walletkernel.jock serves %import-keys, %fakenet, %update-balance-grpc, %list-notes and %create-tx; peeks %fakenet, %state, %signing-keys and %master-signing-key; and emits [%file %write path jam], %markdown and %exit. ⟨lane tools/wallet.sh; lane tools/signer.sh⟩

C-60 · create-tx builds its seeds from the cause's own orders and binds NO PARENT HASH. Parent-hash binding and lock reconstruction are the planner's, which is phase 4 with key derivation. The signature is correct for the spend it signs; giving that spend the right inputs is the next phase's job, and the kernel's markdown says so. ⟨lane tools/signer.sh⟩

7. Fixtures and vectors

C-61 · EVERY FIXTURE CHECKS AGAINST THE VENDORED ENGINE ITSELF, building a value in Jock and the expected one in Hoon typed by the engine's own mold, compared noun for noun. Four families are live in tools/molds.sh — mold, id, treap-shape and quirk — and run per push. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh; lane ci:pins⟩

C-62 · AN EXPECTED VALUE IS THE ENGINE'S BUNT: canonical, shape-revealing, needing no constructor, and impossible to transcribe wrongly by hand — which is the failure mode a hand-written fixture has. A mold whose noun differs reports MOLD MISMATCH, and one that stops compiling fails hard. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh⟩

C-63 · MOLD-FIDELITY vectors are Jock-constructed values noun-equal to tx-engine-constructed expected values, computed IN the harness by the vendored engine — the mkey pattern, where the expected treap is built by the same door the wire uses. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh⟩

C-64 · ID vectors compute digests by FUSING hash-hashable:tip5. The DSL is a description of a hash and every structure is known where it is written, so each arm applies the reduction rules directly instead of building a $hashable for an interpreter to take apart. The reduction is compositional — a pair's digest is hash-ten-cell of its parts' — so fusing arm by arm is sound, and it means no Hashable type is needed. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh⟩

What blocks declaring Hashable is no longer its $^ case, which C-24 spells. It is the other half of that sentence, a separate limitation that outlives the first: a recursive untagged union cannot be declared from source at all.

C-65 · A ZMAP NODE IS [[key value] [left right]] AND A ZSET NODE [element [left right]], and the Jock ZMap/ZSet nominals erase to exactly those — so the walks destructure the real treap rather than a copy. ⟨t-cimp-match; lane tools/molds.sh⟩

C-66 · compute-id agrees on an empty, a one-entry and a TWO-ENTRY spends map. The two-entry case is the one that matters: swapping the walk's left and right recursion left the one-entry fixtures green, a one-node treap having no left and right to swap. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh⟩

C-67 · The two containers holding OPAQUE values hash through hoon.iscell. A seed's note-data and a witness's hax both need hashable-noun, which walks an arbitrary noun and so needs a runtime cell test. Jock had none — ?(e) was cut (language.md L-88) and %cell-check stayed compiler-internal — so the fix was an ABI arm rather than a language change, hoon.iscell being how Jock reaches every other substrate primitive. The surface ruling stands untouched. ⟨t-abi-iscell-cell, t-abi-iscell-atom; lane tools/molds.sh⟩

C-68 · page v1 is a plain TWELVE-FIELD RECORD, the $^ version dispatch living in the combined engine rather than in v1's own core — so it can be declared and both of its hashes computed: hashBlockCommitment, a nine-element hashable committing to neither the page's own digest nor its pow, and computeDigest, which is the block id. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh⟩

C-69 · THE POW SLOT IS THE ONE THING JOCK DOES NOT COMPUTE, and that is a decision. H(proof) is hash-proof, which absorbs STARK proof objects into a sponge. So the proof digest is an argument to computeDigest, and the present-proof branch is pinned by handing it one and checking the structure around it against the engine's own hashable. A candidate page — the kind a Jock program actually builds — has no proof and needs nothing. Hashing a proof would be the only dishonest arm in the file. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh⟩

C-70 · AN ATOM AT OR ABOVE THE OXFOI PRIME IS NOT A BELT (2^64 − 2^32 + 1) and crashes the engine, so a fixture's leaves must stay under it. ⟨t-as-grd-sub; lane tools/molds.sh⟩

C-71 · TREAP-SHAPE vectors build the same containers in OPPOSITE INSERTION ORDERS and compare NOUNS, not digests. Ordering is consensus. Deletion is included because that is where a treap rebalances, and a rebalance that disagreed would still hash consistently on each side while diverging from the wire; so are the two idempotence claims — re-putting a key at its existing value, deleting an absent one — and zkey. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh⟩

C-72 · THE QUIRK LEDGER pins deliberate legacy rather than design, so a future upstream "fix" reddens the lane instead of silently changing consensus. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh⟩

  1. The nname dead pact slot. A name is [first last ~]; the third slot is the residue of a pact mechanism that no longer exists, is always ~, and is still hashed. Jock carries it as pad rather than pretending a name is a pair (C-30).
  2. lock-merkle-proof-stub's hardcoded legacy digest — upstream's "standard library hash (stopgap for determinism)", preserved and never fixed. The sharp row is the axis-blindness: the Jock stub hash at axis seven equals the engine's at axis one, which is only true because the stub hashable does not commit to the axis at all — the defect the %full case exists to correct (C-50).
  3. The two fee-counting regimes around bythos-phase. Below the activation height, base-fee is doubled, seed words are counted per-spend, and inputs pay undiscounted; at or above it, base-fee halves back, note-data merges by lock-root before counting, and inputs pay 1/input-fee-divisor. The same spends map has two different minimum fees — 557.056 and 131.072 — and the boundary is exact: 53.999 is legacy, 54.000 is bythos.
  4. coinbase-split's two-round-then-dust remainder. After two proportional rounds the leftover atoms go to the first key in treap order: 100 over three equal shares is 34/33/33, not 33/33/33 with a coin lost; 7 is 3/2/2.
  5. The $^ vs $% version-dispatch inconsistency, one row per type. Three shapes coexist: spend dispatches on a numeric head tag ($%), nnote and lock-merkle-proof on the head's shape ($^), and raw-tx and page v1 do not dispatch at all, carrying a fixed version field.

C-73 · A $% FORK BUNTS TO ITS MUG-FIRST CASE, not its source-first one. So *spend is the %1 case though %0 is written first, and every fixture leaning on a $% bunt leans on that. ⟨lane tools/molds.sh⟩

C-74 · NOTHING YET PROVES AN EMITTED TRANSACTION IS THE ONE THAT WAS PLANNED. Everything here proves a signature is right for a digest. The check this corpus most lacks is tx-driver's validate_signed — a signed transaction against the plan that asked for it, covering signatures, inputs, outputs and fee together — and it lives on a branch, not master (C-17). ⟨none — UNPINNED⟩

8. Resolved questions

The six questions this section used to carry as open all have answers, written under them as sub-bullets. They are rules now; the questions and their deliberation are in docs/record/chain-arc.md.

C-75 · The snapshot commit is a MASTER ANCESTOR, dated and recorded, picked at vendoring time rather than tracking a branch. ⟨lane ci:pins⟩

C-76 · The printer reports PROOF PRESENCE ONLY. pow stays opaque — * or a jammed @ — the proof is opaque to the kernel, and the kernel never needs to know its size. Presence plus bit-length would be free; STARK internals are out of scope indefinitely. This changes when the kernel gains proof-support capabilities; for now the printer is a read-only tool. ⟨lane tools/watch.sh⟩

C-77 · ZMap/ZSet registration travels with the chain package's def-env fragment, not the boot surface (C-38). ⟨t-cimp-nest, t-cimp-annot⟩

The longer-term shape, ruled workable but not built. Treap containers become instances of an ORDERING-PARAMETERIZED mechanism: the ordering pair (gor, mor) attaches to the nominal at declaration as a def-env fact — no dependent types, just declaration-time constants of noun type (*, *) -> Bool — and put/get/del/key derive from ONE shared treap algebra instantiated per nominal. zoon.hoon being Hoon's map code with the comparators swapped is the existence proof that one algebra serves both.
